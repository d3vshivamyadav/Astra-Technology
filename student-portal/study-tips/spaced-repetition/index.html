What is Spaced Repetition?

Spaced repetition is a learning technique that involves reviewing material at increasing intervals over time. Instead of cramming, you revisit information just when you're about to forget it — strengthening memory with each review.

"Spaced repetition is the most powerful learning technique ever discovered." — Dr. Piotr Wozniak

This technique leverages the spacing effect — the phenomenon where information is better retained when learning sessions are spaced out over time.

The Forgetting Curve & Spaced Repetition

The Ebbinghaus Forgetting Curve shows that we lose 50% of what we learn within 1 hour, and up to 80% within 30 days — without review.

Without review → 80% forgotten in 30 days
With spaced repetition → 80% retained in 30 days
Spaced repetition works by reviewing material at the peak of the forgetting curve — just when you're about to forget it. This strengthens the memory and moves it to long-term storage.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Cramming instead of spacing — Cramming leads to short-term memory. Spacing leads to long-term memory.
Reviewing too early — Reviewing before you start to forget wastes effort. Wait for the optimal interval.
Reviewing too late — If you wait too long, you'll have to re-learn the material completely.
Not using active recall — Reviewing should be active, not passive. Test yourself, don't just re-read.
Overloading with too many cards — Quality over quantity. Focus on what matters most.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the ideal spaced repetition interval?
The optimal intervals depend on the material, but a common schedule is: 1 day → 3 days → 7 days → 14 days → 28 days. Apps like Anki use algorithms to adjust intervals based on your performance.
How does spaced repetition compare to cramming?
Cramming works for short-term recall (like the next day's exam) but leads to rapid forgetting. Spaced repetition is for long-term retention — the knowledge stays with you for months or years.
Can I use spaced repetition for language learning?
Absolutely! It's the most effective method for learning vocabulary, grammar, and pronunciation. Apps like Anki and Memrise are built specifically for language learning with spaced repetition.
How many cards should I review per day?
Start with 20-30 cards per day. As you build the habit, you can increase to 50-100 cards. The key is consistency — daily reviews are more important than the number of cards.
What if I miss a review day?
Don't worry! Just pick up where you left off. The spaced repetition algorithm will adjust the intervals based on your performance. Consistency is more important than perfection.
Can spaced repetition be combined with other study techniques?
Yes! Spaced repetition works best with active recall, the Feynman technique, and interleaving. Use them together for maximum learning efficiency.
